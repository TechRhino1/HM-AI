# Trade-plan audit — full findings (2026-09-15/16)

Detailed audit record. MEMORY.md carries only the 3-line pointer. Read this when working on
signal quality, sizing, or the P0/P1/P2 remediation list.

## Headline: the entry signal has no measured edge

Measured on real MT5 bars, 20 symbols, SWING/H1 (and re-measured at 365d — see bottom).

* PF **0.568–1.202**; **1 of 40** symbol×target combos reaches the 1.3 acceptance bar. Win rate
  28.2–44.8% where a 1.5R target needs 40.0% to break even.
* At ~1% risk/trade, DD is 62–100%. DD ≤10% needs **0.012–0.108% risk per trade** — below the
  broker minimum lot on most symbols.
* Ruled out **by measurement**: costs (free execution still loses), exit geometry (best == seed),
  target width (PF invariant to tp), candidate ranking (no quantile breaks even), learned filter
  (AUC 0.481). What remains is the directional call.

### The directional call is 100% hand-authored

* 7-branch if/elif on `choch`/`bos`/`trend_score` — `decision_engine.py:105-122`.
* Gate "probability" = `0.45 ×` a hand-typed 6-bin table that *inflates* its inputs
  (`confidence.py:13-20`) `+ 0.55 ×` a hand-authored linear score clipped to [0.35, 0.88]
  (`online_ml_predictor.py:564-573, 417`).
* Verified 2026-09-16: **0% fitted**. `self.weights = DEFAULT_WEIGHTS.copy()` (literals),
  `bias = 0.20`, no `.fit()`, no persisted weights (`data/models/` holds only the meta-labeler).

### P0-1: do NOT wire up the "already-fitted" `ScoreCalibration`

The obvious fix is wrong. Measured on `config/winrate_profiles.json` (16 symbols):

* n is only **41–126 across 8 bins** (~5–16 trades/bin).
* The fit is **in-sample** and was done on the **cost-free backtest**.
* In-sample Brier ≈ `p(1-p)` (base-rate Brier) for nearly all symbols — the map extracts almost
  nothing. 2/16 collapse to a pure constant under PAV (AUDUSD: all 8 bins = 0.6374).
* Base rates are inflated by the broken cost model (AUDUSD 0.6374 vs ~0.28–0.45 at honest costs).

`ScoreCalibration` lives in `jarvis/intelligence/winrate_targeting.py` (line 81;
`isotonic_calibrate` at 249, fitted at 1240; `WRProfileStore` at 1195) — **not** in
`jarvis/backtesting/`.

Refit on honest 365d cost-bearing data: **0/20 symbols skillful**. Decisive evidence is the
*movement* as n doubled — mean |AUC−0.5| shrank **0.0353 → 0.0211**, tails collapsed
(GBPJPY 0.584→0.480, NZDUSD 0.417→0.496). Real signal persists or sharpens with more data;
noise regresses toward chance. This is noise.

## Live-execution defects (acct 101059540)

* **Account is named "Demo Account"** in MT5 (`/api/diagnostics`) — LIVE mode, live logic, demo
  money. Confirm in-terminal before assuming real capital is at risk.
* **FIXED (`955d40c`) — `sl_price` and `risk_dist` disagreed by ~7×.** `dynamic_levels.py` floored
  `risk_dist` at `pip_size * 5` but never widened `sl_price` to match. The sizer prices risk off
  `sl_price` while the post-fill re-anchor (`execution_engine.py:95`) uses `sl_distance`, so a
  0.7-pip stop was sized at 0.69 lots and then re-anchored to 5 pips on an already-filled
  position: realised 4.6% against a 0.61% target. The floor now applies to the **distance**
  before `sl_price` is derived, and is dynamic — `max(3 × live spread, 10% ATR)`. Guarded by
  `test_sl_price_and_risk_dist_describe_the_same_level`.
* Both stops landed ~5 pips against a 2.0-pip spread — ~40% of the risk distance was spread
  cost. `execution_engine.py` still cannot resize after fill, so it now **warns** when the
  broker's minimum widens the stop past 1.25× the plan.
* **The sizer rejects the only symbols with edge.** WTI refused at `position_sizing.py:96-105`:
  "Minimum lot size (0.01) would force 3.29% risk (target 0.61%)". It then traded EURUSD +
  AUDUSD — the two worst-measured symbols. On a small account the lot grid, not the edge,
  decides what trades.
* `SR_RATCHET` fires every ~5s with 0.2-pip SL moves; MT5 answers "No changes" — no-op chatter.

## P0-1 measured properly (2026-09-17) — and the SELL-spread defect that invalidated run 1

## P0-3 stage A — the stop's noise band (2026-09-17)

`tools/p0_3_exit_geometry_measurement.py --parts A`. Pure OHLC, so it runs without candidates.

`noise = |close−open| / ATR` (causal: bar *i* uses ATR as of *i−1*) is **symbol-invariant and
timeframe-invariant** — body median 0.322–0.373 across FX, JPY crosses, gold, silver, oil, three
indices and three cryptos; adverse q90 = 1.431 (H1) / 1.428 (M15) / 1.406 (M5).

**False-stop rate** = P(the bar's own adverse range > k×ATR), **directional** form (mean of the
up-side and down-side rates — the honest figure for a balanced book):

| k | H1 | M15 | M5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.85 | 16.9% | 16.9% | 17.2% |
| 1.00 | 12.2% | 12.1% | 12.2% |
| **1.11** | **10.0%** | **10.0%** | **10.0%** |
| 1.25 | 7.3% | 7.1% | 7.0% |

One universal `k ≈ 1.11` buys a 10% false-stop rate on every timeframe and symbol. Against it:

* **SCALP `min(0.65×ATR, …)` → 28.3% — the only materially broken cap**
  (`dynamic_levels.py:174,303`). A quarter of M5 bars take it out on their own range, before any
  directional information is used. Because the band is timeframe-invariant, the cap cannot be
  defended as "tighter because the horizon is shorter".
* DAY_TRADING index `0.90` → 15.3%; forex `1.05` → 11.1%; other `1.30` → 6.6%.
* SWING fallback `0.85–1.00×ATR + 0.35 buffer` = 1.20–1.35 → 8.3% / 6.1% — **fine as shipped**.

**Do not implement the spec's "scale by ATR percentile" clause.** corr(rolling k, ATR percentile)
is positive in **20/20** symbols (median +0.234) so the effect is real, but worth only +5.2% —
rolling q90 k moves 1.033 → 1.087 bottom-to-top ATR quintile, ≈1pp of false-stop rate.

**Method trap:** taking `max(up, down)` is the worst case for *any* stop, but a BUY stop sits
below and a SELL stop above. The worst-case form inflated SCALP **28.3% → 53.7%** (1.9×). Both are
computed; only the directional one is quoted. An overstated defect number is as damaging as an
understated one.

Changing the SCALP cap still has to clear the **payoff** test (parts B/C/D) — a lower false-stop
rate is not by itself evidence of a better system.

**Method notes (both were my own errors, both changed the answer):**

1. **The control was biased.** `forced_long_benchmark` entered the long at the candidate's stored
   `fill` — the ask for a BUY row, the **bid** for a SELL row — handing always-long a free
   half-spread and biasing *against* the gate. Corrected to `fill + 2 × spread` for SELL rows.
   Verified the 2× empirically against `fills.entry_fill` (which charges a SELL at `bid − spread` to
   recover the missing exit spread, so the ask really is `fill + 2×spread`). Worth +0.0988R on

2. **Part D overstated cost 10×.** MT5's `spread` column is in **points**; multiplying the bars' raw

   candidates' own `spread_pips` column, which `signal_scan._spread_for_bar` already converts as
   `raw × point_size / pip_size`. **The scanner was right; my tool was wrong.**

`tools/deflated_sharpe_report.py` → `reports/deflated_sharpe_365d.json` /

**The search was never the main problem.** Deflating for the tp grid alone still leaves five symbols

## The `UNKNOWN`-on-`LIVE_MT5` decision (2026-09-17)

The broker-clock fail-open (below) was fixed by repairing the offset. That left the *shape* of the
defect in place: `first_stale_frame` blocked on `STALE` alone, on the stated assumption that
`UNKNOWN` meant "synthetic/fallback frame". It does not — `classify_bar_freshness` also returns
`UNKNOWN` when `last_bar_epoch` is absent or the age is more negative than one bar. So a frame that
**claims to be real** but whose age cannot be computed was tolerated, which is how a broken offset
silently disabled the gate.

**Decision: a frame stamped `LIVE_MT5` with an unverifiable age must block.** "I have real bars and
cannot say how old they are" is not the same statement as "these bars are synthetic", and only the
second one is safe to reason on.

Implemented as `jarvis/market/data_feed.py:first_untrusted_frame()`, returning
`(role, reason, age)` with `reason` ∈ {`stale`, `unverifiable_age`}. Both decision paths
(`orchestrator.run_cycle_for_symbol`, `server.py` auto-selection) now use it, replacing
`first_stale_frame` — which is kept, unchanged, for callers that only want the narrower question.
`MARKET_CLOSED` never blocks, and `UNKNOWN` on a **non-live** frame is still tolerated, so the
synthetic/dev paths are unaffected. New tests in `tests/test_candle_freshness.py` cover the
fail-open reconstruction, the non-live control, and enforcement at the cycle level.

**Verified with teeth, not asserted:** a scratch harness runs the decision cycle twice on the same
`LIVE_MT5` + unverifiable-age frame, once with the old gate and once with the new — old
`refused=False`, new `refused=True`. The fix changes behaviour rather than a function name.

Note the four tests that neutralise the gate now patch `first_untrusted_frame`; patching the old
name would have left them testing nothing, which is the same class of error as the bug.

### The broker-clock defect, in full (found and fixed 2026-09-16)

The backlog line said it "skews session logic". It is worse than that: it **disabled the
stagnation exits**.

MT5 reports `position.time`, `deal.time` and `symbol_info_tick().time` as seconds on the **broker
server's** clock. XM runs EET/EEST — GMT+2 in winter, GMT+3 in summer — so `mt5_client.py:240`
(`time.gmtime(p.time)`) and `database.py:201` (`datetime.fromtimestamp(t, timezone.utc)`) each put
the value 2-3 hours in the **future**.

`position_monitor._get_position_duration_sec` then computes `now_utc - open_dt`, which came out
**2-3 hours short**, and `max(0.0, ...)` flattened it to a hard **zero for the first three hours of
every position's life**. Because the horizon-adaptive block is gated on `if open_dur_sec > 0:`, the
whole time-decay section was skipped in exactly the window it exists to police:

* SCALP "45 minutes without progress" → could not fire until **3h45m**
* DAY_TRADING 6h stagnation → fired at **9h**
* SWING 36h compression → fired at **39h**
* `_determine_position_style` falls back to duration, so a **25-hour-old position read
  `DAY_TRADING` instead of `SWING`** — and style selects which exit rules and timeframe apply.

**Fix.** `jarvis/data/broker_time.py` derives the offset from the broker's own **freshest tick**
rather than hardcoding it (it moves with the broker's DST, differs per broker — +5:30 for India —
and deriving it survives a server change). Snapped to 30-minute granularity with a 300s tolerance
and a ±12/14h range check, so a **weekend-stale tick is rejected** instead of yielding a nonsense
offset. Cached 600s because each derivation costs an MT5 round-trip per symbol. On failure it
returns 0 — the previous behaviour — and **logs a warning once**, so the degradation is visible.
`mt5_client.get_open_positions` and `database.sync_mt5_history` now stamp true UTC.
The `max(0.0, ...)` clamp in `position_monitor` now **warns** instead of silently zeroing.

`tests/test_broker_time.py` — 13 tests. **Three of them fail on the pre-fix code**, including
`'DAY_TRADING' != 'SWING'` for the 25-hour position. Verified by temporarily restoring the buggy
line and re-running, not by assertion.

## P0-3 part E on the fresh M5 183d cache — the SCALP cap, settled (2026-09-17)

`tools/p0_3_exit_geometry_measurement.py --parts E --tf M5 --window 183`
→ `reports/p0_3_stop_cap_M5_183d.json`. 20/20 symbols, k ∈ {0.65, 0.85, 1.00, 1.11, 1.25, 1.50, 2.00}.

**The 0.65×ATR SCALP cap is mechanically indefensible.** Mean stop-out share at k=0.65 is **84.1%**;
≥95% on 7 symbols (AUDUSD 98.1, USDCHF 98.3, USDCAD 98.8, NZDUSD 99.6, SOLUSD 100.0) — the stop binds
before the thesis can play out. That is a defect independent of any edge question.

**But widening it does not create edge.**

* **0/20 symbols have positive expectancy at ANY k.** The best per-symbol max E is NAS100 −0.011 and
  XAUUSD −0.034; the worst is NZDUSD −0.711.
* **16/20 prefer a k wider than 0.65**; only SOLUSD, US500, USDCAD and USDJPY peak at 0.65, and all
  four have *negative* edge at their own optimum.
* Beats-always-long improves from **8/20 at k=0.65** to **14/20 at each symbol's best k** — but 8/20 is
  already below the 10 expected by chance, and per-symbol verdicts have already been shown to flip on
  window (8/20 flipped when the window was halved), so this is not evidence of skill.

**Verdict: widen the cap, and do not call it a fix.** 84% stop-out is a mechanical defect worth
correcting on its own merits; the reason the system loses money is the entry signal, which part E
re-confirms at every stop width. Any change to the cap must be re-measured on a second window before
it is believed.

## P2-1 extended to M5/SCALP (2026-09-17)

`tools/deflated_sharpe_report.py --tf M5 --window 183` → `reports/deflated_sharpe_M5_183d.json`.
DSR had previously only ever been run on H1; SCALP was untested by the strictest instrument.

**0/20 pass**, by every variant of the test: 0/20 at each symbol's selected `tp_r`, 0/20 at the
production `tp_r` 1.5, and 0/20 on effective n. 17/20 symbols have a **negative** observed SR
(AUDUSD −0.204, GBPUSD −0.225, USDCHF −0.226, SOLUSD −0.287); only NAS100 +0.013, US30 +0.001,
WTI +0.034 and XAUUSD +0.053 are positive, all far below any deflated threshold.

**The book is worse than chance, not merely indistinguishable from it.** 547,615 trades are worth
**1,326 independent bets (0.2%)** — the same overlapping-trade inflation seen on H1, worse in absolute
terms. Deflating across the whole search (n_trials=160) gives **E[max SR] = +0.321** under the null:
picking the best of 160 *random* configurations would be expected to beat the actual book SR of
**−0.197**. SCALP does not just lack edge; random search over the same grid would very likely have
done better.

**Side finding: the sweep is pinned to the grid boundary.** 17/20 symbols select `tp_r` = 6.00, the
largest value in the grid (only NAS100 3.00, SOLUSD 4.00, UK100 1.25 differ). A selection that runs
to the edge of its own search space is not a fitted optimum — the same boundary artefact already
noted for the win-rate objective. It should not be read as "SCALP wants a 6R target".

## Direction audit extended to M15 and M5; DSR extended to M15 (2026-09-17)

Both instruments had previously only ever been run on H1. The three remaining gaps are now closed on
the fully-rescanned cache, and all three agree with H1.

**Direction audit (`--windows 183`), pooled across 20 symbols:**

| tf | pooled n | gate mean R | always-long mean R | **edge over always-long** | gate profitable | beats always-long |

| M5  | 547,615 | −0.2285 | −0.2270 | **−0.0015** | **0/20** | 9/20 |

The gate's edge over always-long is **negative on both** — the directional call *subtracts* value
relative to simply holding long. Beats-always-long is 8/20 and 9/20, both below the 10 expected by
chance. On M5 the gate is profitable on **zero** symbols. The gate is directionally balanced
(buy_pct ≈ 0.48–0.57), so this is not a one-sided bet being compared against the wrong control.

**DSR on M15 183d:** book 182,508 trades → **542 independent bets (0.3%)**, book SR **−0.1281**

to produce. **1/20 symbols pass at their selected `tp_r`, 0/20 on effective n, 0/20 at production
`tp_r` 1.5.**

*That single pass is the false-positive rate.* A 0.95 threshold across 20 symbols is *expected* to
throw one winner by chance; it disappears the moment effective n is used. Worth remembering before
anyone cites a lone survivor as a finding.
