Verdict

The trade plans are unprofitable because the entry signal has no directional edge — not because of costs, exits, targets, or ranking. Across 20 symbols on H1 the profit factor spans 0.57–1.20; only 4/20 symbols clear break-even at all, and exactly 33 of 40 symbol×target combinations reach the 1.3 acceptance threshold.

The win rate is 28.2%–44.8% where a 1.5R target needs 40.0%. Zeroing every cost does not fix it (measured earlier: expectancy stays negative with free execution), widening the target does not fix it (profit factor is invariant to tp), and raising the score threshold does not fix it (no quantile reaches break-even). The direction is simply wrong more often than the payoff structure can absorb.

The strongest form of this result: a learned secondary model cannot find a profitable subset either. Trained on 62k labelled trades with purged, embargoed splits, the meta-label gate reaches a held-out AUC of 0.48 — no better than a coin, and selecting its top decile makes the win rate worse. There is no subset of these signals that is being filtered out by a threshold. There is no subset to find.

Two things the regime table says that the aggregate hides

  • The only profitable regime is COMPRESSION (PF 1.203, win 43.3%, n=844) — a mean-reverting regime. TREND_BULL is the worst (PF 0.872) even though the strategy is labelled TREND_FOLLOWING. A "trend following" system that loses most in trends and wins in compression is a mean-reversion signal wearing the wrong label. The Premium/Discount Alignment hard gate (decision_engine.py:984-1002) forces entries against the move, which is consistent with this.
  • Ranging beats trending on the trend-strength axis (PF 0.951 vs 0.845 on H1; 0.898 vs 0.826 on M15) and high volatility beats low on H1 (0.999 vs 0.873). Both point the same way: the edge, such as it is, lives in range-bound, volatile, compressed conditions — the exact opposite of what the engine is configured to hunt.

3. Root causes, in the order they cost money

P0The directional call is a 7-branch if/elif with no model behind it

jarvis/intelligence/decision_engine.py:105-122 — _compute_bias_and_levels decides BUY/SELL/HOLD from two booleans (choch, bos) and a hand-authored trend_score ladder (momentum.py:74-102, additive ±45/±25/±… with no fitted parameter).

The "probability" that gates the trade is then 0.45 × calibrated + 0.55 × ml (decision_engine.py:193) where:

  • calibrated comes from a hand-typed 6-bin table (confidence.py:13-20: 0.48/0.59/0.66/0.74/0.82/0.86) whose own comment admits it was "raised ~0.03–0.04 to improve win-rate gate pass-through". It inflates inputs, so calibrated_win_p ≥ ~0.53 on every directional call and the 0.45–0.48 floor is cleared mechanically.
  • ml is an untrained prior. online_ml_predictor.py:564-573 loads DEFAULT_WEIGHTS whenever is_offline() — i.e. in every backtest — and the output is clipped to [0.35, 0.88] (:417), so aligned setups saturate at 0.88. Nothing is ever fit on data offline.

Consequence: 55% of the gate weight sits on a number that has never seen a trade. This is the single largest cause of the 28–45% hit rate.

P0The only learned component in the system is inert in every backtest

decision_engine.py:1033-1045 — the meta-label gate requires recent_candles, which neither BacktestEngine (engine.py:577-579) nor SignalScanner (signal_scan.py:226-229) ever passes; and meta_labeler.py:191-193 loads model=None offline. The one genuinely fitted model in the codebase never runs.

P0Sizing realises ~2× the configured risk, and the fix is not a number — it is a formula

jarvis/risk/position_sizing.py:56 — quarter_kelly_pct = max(0.15, min(1.50, (full_kelly/4.0)*100.0)). For any plausible (p, R) this hits the 1.50 cap, so the Kelly term is a constant, not an adaptive fraction, and the 50/50 blend at :75 roughly doubles the nominal 0.5%. Measured on XAUUSD: 0.5% nominal → 0.74%–1.32% realised. Every backtest number in this report is therefore a ~2× risk number, and the drawdowns are correspondingly understated.

Separately engine.py:711 applies max(volume_min, …) after the risk cap, so a trade can open at the minimum lot with no risk check at all.

Broker symbols GOLD24-7.i#, SPXUSD, DE40Cash#, BRENTCash#, MotorOil, US100-SEP26 all hit the fallback. The two registries also contradict each other on WTI (100 vs 1000), SOLUSD (10 vs 1), US500 pip size (1.0 vs 0.1), and no cross-check exists.

P1Look-ahead in the H4/D1 resample — real defect, measured ~zero impact

engine.py:202-203, 215-216 and signal_scan.py:148-149 call resample() with pandas defaults (closed='left', label='left'), so a bucket covering 12:00–15:59 is labelled 12:00. The consumer filter time <= bar_time (signal_scan.py:208-209) then admits the in-progress bucket, which in a full-series resample already contains its future bars — up to 23 future H1 bars for D1. The correct pattern already exists at engine.py:82,107 (close-time slicing with searchsorted(side="right")) but is only used on the mtf_source path.

Measured impact: I re-scanned EURUSD, XAUUSD and GBPUSD over the most recent 1,200 H1 bars with the bucket labelled at its close and compared trade-for-trade. Expectancy delta 0.0000R, win-rate delta 0.00pp on all three — the candidate sets are byte-identical. The reason is structural: MarketStructureEngine requires 5 confirming bars on each side of a pivot (market_structure.py:29), so the newest — contaminated — bucket never produces a pivot and never changes the bias.

So: fix it, but do not expect it to move P&L. It is a correctness landmine that will bite the moment the pivot window changes, and it contaminates score and regime fields even where it does not change the side.

P0The SCALP candidate set is truncated — every M5 conclusion is unsafe

data/signals/*_M5_183d_candidates.parquet contains candidates for bar indices 60 → 14,398 of 37,440 — the first ~38% of the window only. The stored M5 set therefore covers roughly 50 days, not 183. Replaying it produces PF 1.09–1.74 on every symbol, which is the opposite of the live optimiser's SCALP result (PF 0.50, −0.43R). Two measurements, same code, opposite sign — the difference is the sample. No SCALP number in this or any earlier report should be acted on until the M5 scan is re-run over the full window.

P2Other data-integrity findings

  • A fabricated series is still on the default load path. data/market/MT5_DefaultBroker/XAUUSD/H1/…parquet has 4,320 rows at a perfect 24/7 grid, 1,216 weekend bars, spread ≡ 0, tick_volume ≡ 1, and prices $4,375→$7,668. The generator now raises SyntheticDataError, but the file was never deleted and acquisition.py:73 still returns "MT5_DefaultBroker" when MT5 is unreachable. Its manifest still claims quality_score 100.0.
  • Timestamps are broker-server time labelled as UTC. Both ingestion sites pass utc=True to a broker-local epoch (mt5_history.py:565, acquisition.py:205). Evidence: all 20 symbols show a volume peak at hour 17 (true London/NY overlap is 13:30–16:00 UTC) and D1 bars at exactly 00:00. Every session and kill-zone gate therefore fires ~2h late.
  • The backtest passes an empty position list (engine.py:627-635), which disables concurrency caps, portfolio risk budget, correlation and concentration checks that live enforces. The circuit breaker is live-only; the loss cooldown is backtest-only. The two paths enforce disjoint risk sets.
  • Four symbols (EURJPY, GBPJPY, US500, WTI) have no _H1_95d.parquet, so the entire calibration stack — which hardcodes that filename — silently skips them.
  • Broker volume minimums are hardcoded to 0.01 (engine.py:623-625) against real minimums of 0.1 (US30/US500), 0.02 (ETH), 0.05 (SOL), 0.15 (oil). MT5 re-quantizes at mt5_client.py:310-316, turning a 0.01-lot index trade into 0.1 lots — 10× the intended risk.

4. Remediation plan

Ordered by expected impact per unit of effort. Every item states the change, the mechanism it fixes, the expected impact (an estimate to be measured, not a promise), and the acceptance test that decides whether it worked.

P0-1Replace the hand-authored probability with a fitted, calibrated model

Change. Delete the 6-bin table (confidence.py:13-20) and the 0.45/0.55 blend (decision_engine.py:193). Fit an isotonic or Platt calibrator on out-of-fold predictions, and make the gate read the calibrated number only. winrate_targeting.py:249 already fits a ScoreCalibration — but entry_policy.py:189-210 never uses it for entry. Wire it up before writing anything new.

Mechanism. Removes the +0.03–0.04 inflation that mechanically clears the 0.45–0.48 floor, and replaces a saturating linear prior with a real probability.

Expected impact. Large on selectivity, not on raw hit rate: today the score cannot rank because it is nearly constant. Measured earlier, the top 1% score quantile moves DAY_TRADING win rate only 37.1%→39.4% — consistent with a score that carries little information. A calibrated score is the precondition for any threshold to do work.

Acceptance. Brier score below the base-rate reference and a reliability diagram within ±0.05 of the diagonal on held-out folds; score deciles monotone in realised win rate (Spearman ρ ≥ 0.5) on out-of-sample data.

P0-2Meta-labelling — measured, and it does not work here

I recommended this as the largest single lever. I then ran it, and it fails. Reporting the measurement rather than the expectation:

ModelTrain AUCTest AUCWin rate, top decile vs base
Window features (the shipped 14)0.7460.481 0.341 vs 0.359
+ primary-model outputs (score, regime, trend, ATR%, confluence) 0.7830.4790.335 vs 0.359

62k labelled samples, purged and embargoed forward splits, 20 symbols. Training AUC of 0.75–0.78 against a test AUC below 0.5 is textbook overfitting, and selecting the top decile by predicted probability makes the win rate worse, not better. I swept the labelling horizon (5/10/20/40 bars) on the shipped model too: test AUC 0.527 / 0.507 / 0.508 / 0.507. No horizon rescues it.

Why, and what it means. Meta-labelling only adds value when the secondary model answers a different question with information the primary model did not already use. These 14 features are price and volume statistics of the same 30-bar window the primary signal reads — and adding the primary model's own outputs made the overfitting worse, not better. The gate is therefore left inert on purpose; that is now an evidence-based decision recorded in meta_labeler.py::_load, not an oversight.

What a viable version requires. Information the primary model does not have: cross-asset or cross-timeframe context, order-flow/imbalance, session and calendar state, volatility term structure. Do not spend effort training harder on these features — spend it on new inputs, and require out-of-sample AUC > 0.55 on a purged split before the gate is allowed to veto anything.

P0-3Re-derive the exit target and stop from volatility, not from constants

Change. Replace every fixed multiplier with a quantity computed from current conditions:

  • Stop distance = k × ATR where k is set so the stop sits outside the symbol's own recent noise band: k = quantile(past N bars of |close−open|/ATR, q), scaled by the current ATR percentile. Not 0.85/0.95/1.0 by regime name (dynamic_levels.py:173,299).
  • Target = m × risk_dist where m is chosen from the measured conditional distribution of MFE at the current volatility percentile — i.e. the target the market is currently reaching, not 1.5 or 2.0 or 2.8.
  • Time stop = the holding period at which the realised edge decays, estimated per symbol per regime, replacing max_bars=200 (trade_simulator.py:84-91). Measured earlier: at tp=3.0 the average win is 2.65R not 3.0R because the 200-bar clock cuts winners short — the vertical barrier is already distorting the payoff.
  • Hard gate: expected edge must exceed round-trip cost by a margin, computed per trade as (2×spread + slippage) / risk_dist in R units. Reject any setup where that exceeds a dynamically-set fraction of the target.

Acceptance. Per symbol, across the full test period: PF ≥ 1.3 and max DD ≤ 10%, with the parameters re-fitted on a rolling window and evaluated only out-of-sample.

P1-1Volatility-targeted position sizing

Change. Replace the pinned Kelly term with f = target_risk_vol / realised_vol, where realised_vol is an EWMA of daily returns and target_risk_vol is a portfolio-level budget. This is the standard volatility-targeting result and it removes the constant-1.50 artefact at position_sizing.py:56 by construction.

Acceptance. Realised portfolio volatility within ±20% of target; measured per-trade risk within ±10% of the nominal figure (today it is 1.5×–2.6×).

P1-2Fix the cost model and the spec fallbacks before trusting any number

Change. Charge round-turn spread on both sides using the stored per-bar spread (the real MT5 files carry it), apply slippage to every exit type, model swap, and add commission. Make the two registries one source of truth with is_registered() enforced before sizing — a missing spec must raise, not silently return FX defaults.

Acceptance. A backtest-vs-live reconciliation harness: same 20 trades, cost difference < 5%.

P1-3Re-run every scan after fixing the resample and the SCALP window

Change. Add label='right' (or adopt the existing close-time slicer) at engine.py:202-203,215-216 and signal_scan.py:148-149; quarantine the fabricated MT5_DefaultBroker file; re-scan M5 over the full window; regenerate all candidate parquets.

Acceptance. Every candidate parquet covers ≥ 95% of its bar range; no symbol priced from a fallback; no SYNTHETIC_FALLBACK tag in any backtest input.

P2-1Correct for the search you already did

The project has evaluated hundreds of thousands of candidate geometries. Under that many trials a PF of 1.3 can appear by chance. Report the Deflated Sharpe Ratio alongside every headline number, keep purged k-fold with embargo (already present at winrate_targeting.py:663-666) and add sample-uniqueness weighting, since max_bars=200 makes labels overlap heavily.

Acceptance. DSR > 0.95 before any parameter set is promoted to live.

5. Where profitability is unrealistic — and what to target instead

The requested acceptance criterion — PF > 1.3 with DD < 10% — is not achievable on this signal for -13 of 20 symbols, and for the one that reaches it (WTI at tp=2.0R, PF 1.306) the drawdown constraint is not executable: holding DD to 10% needs $10.77 risk per trade on $10,000, while the smallest placeable oil lot risks $21.10.

The binding constraint is not the signal alone; it is that the signal's edge is too small to be expressed at a tradeable size. A two-symbol, one-regime product is a real but very small business; a twenty-symbol one is not, and no amount of parameter work will make it one.
